SADL: A Size-Adaptive Decision Layer for Chronic Stroke Lesion Segmentation in ISLES 2026
Authors: Marek Wodzinski, Gniewosz Drwiega, Wojciech Szymanski
First: 2026-10-09T11:28:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T11:28:58+00:00
Comments: ISLES 2026 Challenge Contribution
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Chronic ischemic stroke lesions are difficult to segment on T1-weighted MRI. Their appearance overlaps with healthy tissue, and their volumes span four orders of magnitude, so voxel-level and lesion level metrics often disagree. We build an eleven-member nnU-Net ensemble that covers five design axes: a baseline dice + top-K loss, heavy augmentation, per-lesion volume loss re-weighting, stochastic weight averaging, and a sampling-and-architecture block that pairs cohort-balanced sampling with a residual-encoder backbone. Weighted soft-map fusion of the members feeds a tuned decision layer, SADL, that estimates lesion volume, applies an adaptive per-lesion-size threshold, prunes components by voxel count and peak probability, and adds a never-empty rescue. All fusion weights and per-bucket parameters are fitted on out-of-fold (OOF) predictions, so tuning never touches the held-out test set. On a site-disjoint 91-case holdout, SADL reaches Dice 0.7322, lesion-F1 0.7461 and PR-AUC 0.8430 when scored online with the challenge's released evaluation code. A paired non-parametric bootstrap against a reference single ensemble member confirms the lesion-F1 gain at p=0.024, and the gain is largest in the small-lesion regime, where the rescue step recovers cases that a fixed threshold would silently discard.

A4-MR: Abnormality-aware Anatomical Alignment for MR
Authors: Noel D'souza, Zhijian Yang, Nishank Singla, Pranay Doshi, Krisztian Koos, Fei Tan, Bruno Nunes, Laszlo Rusko, Bernadett Kolozsvari, Eszter Sabo, Emanuele Valeriano, Vikash Gupta, Seyed Iman Zare Estakhraji, Marc Lebel, Farzin Haddadpour, Taha Kass-Hout, Erhan Bas
First: 2026-10-09T06:10:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T06:10:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent advances in large-scale foundation models have enabled strong generalization across a wide range of clinical and research tasks in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). However, existing vision-language MRI models remain limited in anatomy-specific localization and pathology-aware representation, particularly in real-world settings. We introduce A4-MR, a 3D vision-language foundation model trained on approximately 50,000 MRI studies comprising 491,000 series, roughly half of which are paired with radiology reports. A4-MR is designed to: (1) learn anatomy localization through large-scale pretraining with noisy labels; (2) align image and text representations at the anatomical level; and (3) learn distinct representations of healthy and abnormal anatomy. Across a diverse set of downstream tasks, A4-MR outperforms existing foundation models, including a +1.7 AUROC improvement over MedSigLIP for abnormality detection. Using a frozen encoder, A4-MR also surpasses an end-to-end task-specific MedNeXt model for knee segmentation by +1.4 Dice and performs comparably to the best task-specific model for abdominal organ segmentation on AMOS, within 0.4 Dice. Finally, without anatomical prompts, A4-MR localizes 3D anatomy comparably to MedSAM2 provided with ground-truth bounding boxes. Together, these results demonstrate that anatomy-level multimodal alignment and abnormality-aware representation learning can enable a single MRI foundation model to support localization, segmentation, and pathology-related tasks.

Component-Adaptive and Lesion-Level Supervision for Improved Small Structure Segmentation in Brain MRI
Authors: Minh Sao Khue Luu, Evgeniy N. Pavlovskiy, Bair N. Tuchinov
First: 2026-04-09T09:15:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T03:02:06+00:00
Comments: v04: This version added evaluation on a second dataset (3D-MR-MS) and a held-out test set; added statistical significance tests and error analysis; added new references; corrected the optimizer description; update figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Small lesions in brain MRI are hard to segment because they occupy a tiny fraction of the volume and are dominated by background and larger lesions during voxel-wise optimization, so a model can reach a high Dice similarity coefficient (DSC) while missing many of them. We propose CATMIL, a training objective that adds two auxiliary terms to the standard nnU-Net Dice and cross-entropy loss without changing the architecture. The Component-Adaptive Tversky (CAT) term weights lesion voxels by the inverse size of their connected component, so each lesion contributes nearly equally regardless of volume. The lesion-level Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) term treats each lesion as a bag of voxels and penalizes lesions with no detected voxel. For multiple sclerosis lesion segmentation on MSLesSeg, CATMIL achieves the highest small-lesion recall (0.873 vs. 0.796 for Dice+CE; 95% CI of the difference +0.030 to +0.157, higher in all six test patients) and about 48% fewer missed lesions, with comparable DSC and HD95. The gain holds for lesions of at least 3 mm in diameter, the clinical reading size (recall 0.944 vs. 0.870). Standard losses produce no probability response to most small lesions they miss, so no threshold can recover them. The cost is more small false-positive components; a simple component-size filter removes most of them while keeping the sensitivity gain, and at matched lesion-wise precision CATMIL detects more small lesions with higher lesion-wise F1. An ablation attributes the detection gain to the MIL term. On a second dataset, 3D-MR-MS, CATMIL with the same loss weights again improves small-lesion recall, at a larger false-positive cost and slightly lower DSC. CORE-Seg: Reasoning-Driven Segmentation for Complex Lesions via Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Yuxin Xie, Yuming Chen, Yishan Yang, Yi Zhou, Tao Zhou, Zhen Zhao, Jiacheng Liu, Huazhu Fu
First: 2026-03-06T04:58:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-09T01:26:23+00:00
Comments: Accepted by Computational Visual Media
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Medical image segmentation is undergoing a paradigm shift from conventional visual pattern matching to cognitive reasoning analysis. Although Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have shown promise in integrating linguistic and visual knowledge, significant gaps remain: existing general MLLMs possess broad common sense but lack the specialized visual reasoning required for complex lesions, whereas traditional segmentation models excel at pixel-level segmentation but lack logical interpretability. In this paper, we introduce ComLesion-14K, the first diverse Chain-of-Thought (CoT) benchmark for reasoning-driven complex lesion segmentation. To accomplish this task, we propose CORE-Seg, an end-to-end framework integrating reasoning with segmentation through a Semantic-Guided Prompt Adapter. We design a progressive training strategy from SFT to GRPO, equipped with an adaptive dual-granularity reward mechanism to mitigate reward sparsity. Our Method achieves state-of-the-art results with a mean Dice of 37.06\% (14.89\% higher than the second-best baseline), while reducing the failure rate to 18.42\%. Lesion-DDPM: Lesion-Enhanced 3D Diffusion for MS MRI Synthesis
Authors: Weidong Zhang, Yongchan Jung, Shafayat Mowla Anik, Furen Xiao, Vasudevan Janarthanan, Enkhzaya Chuluunbaatar, Marco Ho, Byeong Kil Lee, Jeeho Ryoo
First: 2026-06-13T20:21:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-08T18:14:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
3D FLAIR MRI is widely recommended as one of the standard MRI sequences for brain imaging in multiple sclerosis (MS), but publicly available MS datasets remain relatively small and vary across scanners, acquisition protocols, and lesion patterns. This scarcity and variability hinder the development of robust neuroimaging machine learning models and are particularly challenging for generative models that aim to synthesize images while preserving small, sparse lesions. We propose Lesion-DDPM, a 3D conditional diffusion framework for lesion-aware FLAIR synthesis that incorporates multi-level anatomical mask injection together with a lesion-weighted reconstruction loss to emphasize lesion voxels while maintaining global brain structure. Using a curated subset of the MSLesSeg dataset, we compare Lesion-DDPM with representative state-of-the-art GAN- and diffusion-based models, assessing both image-generation metrics and downstream 3D U-Net segmentation. In our experiments, Lesion-DDPM achieved the lowest lesion-region reconstruction error among all methods. In a downstream 3D U-Net lesion segmentation task, a model trained only on Lesion-DDPM-generated scans and evaluated on real MRIs reached a Dice score of 0.616 compared with 0.569 for the best competing synthetic dataset. When Lesion-DDPM images were added to the real training set, the Dice score further increased to 0.685.

标题：Lesion-DDPM：用于MS MRI合成的病灶增强3D扩散模型
3D FLAIR MRI被广泛推荐为多发性硬化（MS）脑成像的标准MRI序列之一，但公开可用的MS数据集仍然相对较小，并且在不同扫描仪、采集协议和病灶模式之间存在差异。这种稀缺性和变异性阻碍了稳健神经影像机器学习模型的开发，并且对于旨在合成图像同时保留小而稀疏病灶的生成模型尤其具有挑战性。我们提出Lesion-DDPM，一种用于病灶感知FLAIR合成的3D条件扩散框架，它结合了多级解剖掩膜注入以及病灶加权重建损失，以在保持全局脑结构的同时强调病灶体素。使用MSLesSeg数据集的精选子集，我们将Lesion-DDPM与代表性的最先进GAN和扩散模型进行比较，评估图像生成指标和下游3D U-Net分割。在我们的实验中，Lesion-DDPM在所有方法中实现了最低的病灶区域重建误差。在下游3D U-Net病灶分割任务中，仅在Lesion-DDPM生成的扫描上训练并在真实MRI上评估的模型达到了0.616的Dice分数，而最佳竞争合成数据集为0.569。当将Lesion-DDPM图像添加到真实训练集时，Dice分数进一步提高到0.685。
Summary / 总结
Publicly available multiple sclerosis (MS) MRI datasets are small and heterogeneous, which hampers the development of robust generative models that must preserve small, sparse lesions. The authors propose Lesion-DDPM, a 3D conditional diffusion framework for lesion-aware FLAIR synthesis that injects multi-level anatomical masks and uses a lesion-weighted reconstruction loss to emphasize lesion voxels while maintaining global brain structure. On a curated subset of MSLesSeg, Lesion-DDPM achieved the lowest lesion-region reconstruction error among compared GAN- and diffusion-based models; a 3D U-Net trained only on its synthetic scans reached a Dice score of 0.616 on real MRIs versus 0.569 for the best competing synthetic dataset, and adding its images to the real training set raised the Dice score to 0.685.
公开的多发性硬化（MS）MRI数据集规模小且异质性高，这阻碍了需要保留细小稀疏病灶的稳健生成模型的发展。作者提出Lesion-DDPM，一种用于病灶感知FLAIR合成的三维条件扩散框架，通过注入多级解剖掩膜并采用病灶加权重建损失来强调病灶体素，同时保持整体脑结构。在MSLesSeg的精选子集上，Lesion-DDPM在对比的GAN和扩散模型中取得了最低的病灶区域重建误差；仅在其合成扫描上训练的3D U-Net在真实MRI上达到0.616的Dice分数，而最佳竞争合成数据集为0.569；将其图像加入真实训练集后，Dice分数进一步提升至0.685。
